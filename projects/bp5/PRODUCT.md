# Working pilot and its limits

The problem is getting useful practice for a real Swedish interaction, then knowing what to practise next. The first customer hypothesis is an adult learning Swedish for daily life, including an SFI learner. Willingness to pay and improved learning are unvalidated.

The pilot connects three original scenes to actual local speech recognition, dialogue generation and Swedish audio. Basic starts with a café request; Medium asks a neighbour for a favour; High discusses a problem with a friend. Chat supports connected reading, writing and recall. Sessions, phrases and evidence persist in a private SQLite database and can be exported or deleted.

Acceptance requires working voice round trips, a complete exchange at each challenge level, related reading/writing, retained history, source/version-aware SFI evidence, visible help use, cancellation and safe retry, and mobile-compatible controls. Checks must distinguish simulated test fixtures, synthetic audio integration, real browser behaviour and human-speaker validation. Do not call an untested capability complete.

Source references establish the official criteria and transition rules. The mappings are partial authored interpretations, not validated assessment. Difficulty, support, course and syllabus version are distinct. Emotional context and later reuse are design hypotheses; no improved-memory claim is established.

Current limitations include incorrect model Swedish, fallible transcription, CPU latency, single-owner access and no physical-phone or human-speaker validation yet. The existing role-play competitors remain relevant. Before a customer offer, compare usefulness and repeated use with existing alternatives, review language/content with an SFI educator and measure actual inference/support costs. Prototype availability is not a product release or business validation.
