Existing T6 forecasting topic — proposed refinement, not a new batch slot
Investigated 2 November 2026. No existing job or topic scope changed.

The October 15 Timestral release and October 29 Winder study make pretrained forecasting a current lead. The Winder study also acknowledges use of actual future weather and possible pretraining exposure. This is useful counterevidence: a hybrid residual model is already an existing approach, and an oracle-weather result is not an operational forecast.

A concrete solo study could freeze the Apache-2.0 Chronos-Bolt Tiny model (9M parameters), train a small residual/quantile adapter using only calendar features and genuinely available past measurements, and evaluate rolling forecast origins on UCI Bike Sharing and optionally household electricity data. Compare seasonal naive, linear autoregression, a tree/covariate model, frozen Chronos, calibration-only and an existing covariate-aware hybrid. Exclude Bike Sharing's casual/registered components when predicting their sum. Future observed weather may appear only as an explicitly labelled oracle upper bound.

The contribution would be a controlled study of how residual adaptation and interval calibration behave when covariate availability is honest, including a negative result. It must not claim invention of hybrid forecasts or pretraining-unseen evaluation. Point/interval errors, coverage, width, horizon, CPU time and seasonal drift should be measured under time-ordered splits. Data terms are CC-BY-4.0; a real Bike Sharing CSV prefix was parsed, while the optional power-data full acquisition has not been tested.
