ST8 — Learn which anomaly detector to trust after one confirmed event
Investigated 2026-11-02 | KEEP | kept_as_investigated_possibility

Different anomaly detectors notice different things: a sudden spike, a lasting level change or unusual timing. A small learned combiner could use experience from other signals and one confirmed event to decide which detectors are useful on a new signal.
Example: After one unusual CPU-usage episode is confirmed, the model should improve later alarms without repeatedly flagging ordinary daily peaks.

What the study would try
Train a small combiner on separate public signals, then give it one confirmed event on a new signal.
Everyday question: Can experience from other signals help it raise better later alarms after one confirmation?
Expected research output: A trained detector combiner and a causal, source-separated alarm evaluation.

Qualification and research depth
Kept as an investigated possibility because the scope combines explicit model adaptation with a justified controlled comparison, grouped evaluation and measurable failure trade-offs. Data access and primary terms were inspected. This is a feasibility judgment, not completed model evidence, a novelty claim or LTU approval.
The student builds a task-specific model from detector outputs, studies transfer across series families, carefully accounts for training labels and time order, and evaluates alarm trade-offs. It does not require a company's incident log or a recruited operator.

Scientific question and hypothesis
Does a cross-series pretrained combiner provide better later-event recall at a fixed false-alarm rate than a fixed detector ensemble after one confirmation on a new series?
A small gating model trained on separate signal families will transfer useful detector-selection behaviour, but gains will shrink for unseen anomaly types and must be evaluated against a globally trained logistic combiner and simple statistical monitoring.

Model adaptation
A fixed pool of approximately six low-cost detectors: rolling median/MAD, EWMA/CUSUM, autoregressive residuals, isolation forest and window-feature nearest neighbours.
Pretrain a small logistic/MLP gating model across training-series episodes; condition it on causally normalised detector scores and the confirmed-event score profile. At deployment, update only a few gate/calibration parameters from one confirmation.
Approximately 1,000–20,000 parameters; no large time-series transformer is required.
Model terms: Newly written combiner; choose permissively licensed detector implementations and pin their versions.

Comparators
- Rolling MAD/EWMA/CUSUM statistical process control.
- Best development-selected single detector, mean/max ensemble and the OneShot-style fixed-pool weighting rule.
- A globally trained logistic combiner with exactly the same training-label access as the proposed gating model.

Evaluation and leakage controls
Hold out complete series and preferably whole source families, with a separate development family/series set. All normalisation and detector fitting on a test series use only its available past. Simulate one confirmation at a predefined point in the first labelled window; score only later windows after confirmation. Count all cross-series pretraining labels. OneShot does not consume the same pretraining supervision, so report that difference rather than presenting it as an equal-supervision comparison.

Ablations
- No cross-series pretraining, no confirmation conditioning and logistic versus nonlinear gate.
- Detector diversity and score-normalisation choice.
- In-family versus held-out-family transfer; same-type versus different-type later events where labels permit.

Measurements
Event recall at fixed false alarms per unit time, precision-recall, NAB score as secondary, delay from benchmark-window start and CPU cost. Benchmark windows do not reveal a precise real-world incident onset; report that limit. Bootstrap series/families rather than time points.

CPU plan and cheaper fallback
Small CSVs and cached detector scores should fit well below 4 GB. Train only the small combiner, with bounded rolling windows. Target minutes per detector/series and hours for the complete preregistered study, verified in a pilot.
Use a regularised logistic gate and fewer inexpensive detectors. If family transfer is unstable, retain a clearly labelled within-family study and negative-transfer result; do not claim universal monitoring readiness.

Closest work and proposed contribution
The October OneShot paper ALREADY investigates corrupted confirmations, temporal order and normal-set contamination. Those proposed gaps were withdrawn after reading its appendices. Its fixed-pool, no-trained-combiner setting motivates the distinct cross-series learned-combiner question here.

Risks and kill test
NAB windows are coarse and several signals are easy for simple methods. The public benchmark does not establish operational incident relevance or user tolerance. Transfer can merely recognise source-family characteristics. Only five source families and few independent labelled events are available; use regularisation and report broad uncertainty rather than treating windows as independent evidence.
Verify enough independent series remain for development and held-out-family evaluation after duplicate/label checks. If one simple statistical method dominates across budgets, report it and reassess the adapter. Reject any pipeline that uses future statistics or treats every point in an event window as an independently correct alarm.
