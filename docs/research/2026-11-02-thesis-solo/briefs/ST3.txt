ST3 — Keep activity recognition working when a phone moves from hand to pocket
Investigated 2026-11-02 | KEEP | kept_as_investigated_possibility

A movement model can confuse sitting, standing and walking when a phone is carried differently. The study would adapt a pretrained motion model using public recordings, without collecting anyone's activity data.
Example: A model trained on a phone held in the hand is tested on a different person's phone in a pocket.

What the study would try
Adapt a pretrained movement model using public recordings from different people and body positions.
Everyday question: Can activity recognition stay useful when a phone moves from a hand to a pocket?
Expected research output: An adapted movement model and a study of which people, devices and positions it transfers to.

What was actually checked
Dataset license, metadata and real sensor sample verified; no model experiments.
Decoded real accelerometer rows; full README states m/s² and nanosecond timestamps. All 5,231 metadata rows read; body/activity participant support counted. A complete 473,670-byte accelerometer member passed ZIP CRC and parsed 10,996 numeric rows with increasing timestamps, spanning 110.15 seconds at about 99.82 Hz.
Model experiments completed: 0. Runtime targets below are planned limits, not measured results.

Qualification and research depth
Kept as an investigated possibility because the scope combines explicit model adaptation with a justified controlled comparison, grouped evaluation and measurable failure trade-offs. Data access and primary terms were inspected. This is a feasibility judgment, not completed model evidence, a novelty claim or LTU approval.
This is a transfer-learning study with a real device/position hierarchy, competing signal-processing and pretrained-model baselines, labelled/unlabelled adaptation-budget controls and subject-level uncertainty. It examines where pretrained wearable representations transfer and where they fail.

Scientific question and hypothesis
How much does a small adaptation of a frozen wearable encoder improve activity recognition under simultaneous participant and body-position shift?
Unit-consistent preprocessing, rotation augmentation and a small learned feature adapter will improve worst-position macro-F1 over a plain frozen-encoder head. Any target-position adaptation data must come from separate development participants.

Data access and rights
AICOS-HAR; PAMAP2 as optional external check
AICOS-HAR public ZIP supports selected-member range reads. Its metadata names participant, activity/trial, device, position and fold. No private UK Biobank raw data are needed.
AICOS-HAR: 5,231 acquisitions, 106 participant IDs, 101.4 hours; 2.54 GB total. Archive metadata verified Hand/Leg/Hip coverage of 88/55/27 participants. For walking/sitting/standing, the least-covered selected position/activity has 11 participants. Plan a selected 5–15-hour accelerometer subset, not all sensors.
Terms: AICOS-HAR CC-BY-4.0 confirmed in primary Zenodo metadata. PAMAP2 also CC-BY-4.0, but its legacy server ignored range requests; full fallback acquisition is not yet tested.
Still to verify: Audit device-position confounding, original folds versus person IDs, sampling irregularity and target-unit contract. Define a three-activity pilot before expanding.

Model adaptation
HARNET five-second frozen encoder, pretrained on wearable movement; input contract is 3 axes by 150 samples at 30 Hz.
Train a linear/MLP classifier plus a low-rank feature adapter. Compare source-only rotation augmentation with covariance alignment using a strictly separate unlabelled target-position development pool.
Train small heads/adapters; keep the backbone frozen. Exact feature width and checkpoint footprint must be measured in the pilot.
Model terms: HARNET academic/noncommercial license permits a research-scoped study but does not support a commercial-rights claim.

Comparators
- Time/frequency summary features with logistic regression or random forest.
- Small CNN trained from scratch on the same labelled training windows.
- Frozen HARNET plus linear head; rotation augmentation without a learned adapter.

Evaluation and leakage controls
Split participants before windowing; hold entire trials together. Create held-out position and held-out device-family tests, reporting confounding if they cannot be separated. Distinguish zero-target-data adaptation from a fixed budget of unlabelled target-position data supplied by development participants. No test-person windows may set normalization, alignment or thresholds.

Ablations
- Resampling/unit/gravity treatment.
- Rotation augmentation versus learned adapter.
- Source-only versus permitted unlabelled target data; axis magnitude alone versus full axes.

Measurements
Macro-F1, balanced accuracy, worst-position and per-activity recall, calibration and coverage, CPU throughput and RAM. Bootstrap people, not overlapping windows. Report the smallest subgroup rather than hiding it in an overall average.

CPU plan and cheaper fallback
Start with at most 10,000 nonoverlapping five-second windows. Raw 3x150 float32 arrays are about 18 MB; cached embeddings add modest storage. Target at most 6 GB RAM, one overnight encoder pass and small-head fitting on CPU. Runtime is not measured yet.
A compact one-dimensional CNN with the same augmentation/alignment protocol, or frozen-feature normalization only. This remains an Applied AI study even if HARNET transfer fails.

Closest work and proposed contribution
The October AICOS-HAR paper specifically concerns diverse devices and body positions, so position-invariant HAR is not claimed new. The contribution is a controlled, limited-compute adaptation study with combined shifts, explicit data budgets and a strong hand-engineered baseline.

Risks and kill test
Position and device may be confounded; healthy controlled activities do not establish performance for falls, illness or everyday unrestricted use. HARNET's source-body location and unit expectations can dominate transfer.
Build subject-disjoint walking/sitting/standing splits with at least ten target participants in each selected position; inspect resampling and units on several acquisitions. Revise to fewer positions if support is insufficient. Stop the pretrained branch if the checkpoint cannot fit or a small embedding run exceeds the CPU budget.
