ST1 — Tag sound recordings even when their filenames are misleading
Investigated 2026-11-02 | KEEP | kept_as_investigated_possibility

People collect recordings with names such as final2.wav or tags copied from another file. A sound organiser should still recognise the recording and admit when the available clues disagree.
Example: A clip called birds.wav contains a door closing. The system should rely on the sound, or use a broad label, instead of confidently repeating the filename.

What the study would try
Teach a small model when to trust the recording and when to trust its description, then test whether it can safely use a broader label.
Everyday question: Can it still label recordings reliably when descriptions are missing or misleading?
Expected research output: A trained small labeling model and evidence about when it should give a precise label, a broad label or no answer.

What was actually checked
Source, license, archive and sample-feature investigation only. No model was trained.
Decoded one 512-dimensional float32 NPY, read all metadata rows, counted 2,481 uploader IDs, and checked archive members. Sample sound 28570 is CC-BY-4.0.
Model experiments completed: 0. Runtime targets below are planned limits, not measured results.

Qualification and research depth
Kept as an investigated possibility because the scope combines explicit model adaptation with a justified controlled comparison, grouped evaluation and measurable failure trade-offs. Data access and primary terms were inspected. This is a feasibility judgment, not completed model evidence, a novelty claim or LTU approval.
The work trains a multimodal classifier and reliability gate, justifies corruption assumptions, compares strong released-feature baselines, and studies how grouping and hierarchy change conclusions. The deliverable is evidence about representation adaptation and uncertainty, with a functioning example as supporting work.

Scientific question and hypothesis
Under uploader-disjoint evaluation, can a small learned gate between frozen audio and text representations retain useful classification accuracy when metadata is absent or contradictory?
A small conflict-trained gate may improve error-versus-coverage under missing or contradictory metadata relative to published attention fusion and conservative parent gating, at a smaller parameter budget. Existing methods may match or beat it; this is not a measured or novel-method claim.

Data access and rights
BSD10k v1.2
No login. Official metadata and precomputed CLAP audio/text embeddings are separate from the large audio archive.
10,956 clips, 35.25 hours; 49,346,956-byte feature ZIP and 2,726,927-byte metadata ZIP. Initial CC0/CC-BY subset has 9,304 clips across all 23 second-level classes; audio ZIP is 8.58 GB and is unnecessary for the main study.
Terms: Metadata: CC-BY-4.0. Audio has item-specific CC0, CC-BY, CC-BY-NC and Sampling+ licenses. Initial subset excludes NC and Sampling+; retain item attribution and feature provenance.
Still to verify: Count eligible examples per uploader/grouped split; inspect metadata-label shortcuts and exact/near duplicates. Frozen CLAP pretraining exposure is not established as absent.

Model adaptation
Official pretrained CLAP audio and text features; no foundation-model training or waveform inference required.
Train audio-only/text-only linear heads, a concatenation head, then a small gating network using modality disagreement and missingness. Use a hierarchical objective that can return a broad class when detailed confidence is weak. Test against existing attention fusion and conservative parent-consistent gating; extra hierarchy is an ablation rather than an assumed improvement.
Approximately 10,000–150,000 learned head/gate parameters; exact count fixed after feature/schema audit.
Model terms: Use the official released feature route and preserve dataset attribution. Any later direct CLAP checkpoint use requires its own pinned model/license check.

Comparators
- Majority/top-level frequency and metadata-only text classification.
- Audio-only logistic regression and concatenation of the same frozen features.
- Simple probability averaging, fixed metadata dropout, and calibrated hierarchical fallback.
- Published HATR attention-fusion baseline and a conservative audio-parent-consistent metadata gate. Use a clean implementation if the official baseline code license remains unresolved; do not silently omit these strong alternatives.

Evaluation and leakage controls
Group by uploader before splitting; keep every clip and its variants together. Reserve a final uploader-held-out test partition. Tune on development uploaders only. Test clean, text-absent and deliberately mismatched text conditions separately, labelling the latter as controlled stress tests rather than observed prevalence. Hold out at least one corruption construction from adapter tuning so it is not evaluated only on the exact mismatch generator used in training.

Ablations
- Gate versus fixed fusion with identical feature access.
- Training with/without dropout and conflict augmentation.
- Fine-class versus broad-class fallback. For a raw-text shortcut diagnostic, compare TF-IDF metadata heads with and without class-name words; do not pretend precomputed CLAP vectors changed when words are removed.

Measurements
Macro-F1 and balanced accuracy over 23 classes; top-level accuracy; worst-class recall; Brier score and error-versus-coverage curves; CPU latency and peak memory. Bootstrap uploader groups, not individual correlated clips.

CPU plan and cheaper fallback
Feature arrays for 9,304 examples with two 512-float vectors are about 38 MB before metadata/overhead. Head fitting should be modest on a 16 GB CPU laptop; allow a measured pilot to set the experiment budget. Initial experiment cap: 2 GB RAM and one hour per head configuration; no GPU/API. The published attention baseline is larger (about 7.3M trainable parameters); measure its CPU training separately and retain it as the existing-method comparator.
Use audio-only logistic regression plus a learned confidence head. This still permits the grouping/calibration study if the text embedding pathway is incomplete.

Closest work and proposed contribution
The full October DCASE report already describes metadata gates, text dropout and uploader-disjoint evaluation. These are required comparators, not claimed inventions. The contribution is a controlled reliability/coverage/parameter-budget study under fixed representations and matched data, separating clean, missing and contradictory metadata; a negative result is valid.

Risks and kill test
Artificially shuffled filenames may be easier to detect than naturally bad descriptions. Uploader grouping may expose a large benchmark shortcut. Feature pretraining overlap limits claims about completely unseen audio.
Before model work, form a usable grouped split with adequate class support and matching audio/text features. Run a small clean/dropout comparison. Revise if the apparent advantage disappears against fixed fusion or if rare classes have too few independent uploaders; report a negative result rather than inventing a product gain.
