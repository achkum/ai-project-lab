ND6: Stop sleep-stage smoothing from erasing short real changes
4 November 2026 — REVISE

Problem
Making predictions look smoother can improve average accuracy while deleting short bouts that the human scorer actually marked.

Example
A scorer marks one minute of waking between longer sleep periods. A smoother may erase it as noise; the study asks when that helps and when it hides evidence.

Research question
Can a compact learned smoothing gate improve macro-F1 without materially reducing recall of human-labelled short stage bouts, compared with fixed-duration and confidence-aware smoothing?

What you would build/train
Use YASA as a task-specific pretrained LightGBM baseline, plus a locally trained feature classifier. Train a small gate deciding whether to accept an epoch prediction or a smoothed alternative using local probabilities and signal-quality features.

Comparisons
Unsmoothed epoch classifier; majority/median smoothing HMM/HSMM duration smoothing StageGuard-style confidence/quality-aware smoothing; random-transformer smoother where reproducible

Evaluation
Group all nights by person. Tune on development people, test once on held-out people; disclose potential pretrained data exposure. Report macro-F1, transition precision/recall, duration-stratified bout recall and temporal displacement. Score 30-second labels only: no claim to identify micro-arousals absent in annotations. YASA uses future context, so compare offline scoring at matched context windows.

Data and terms
Sleep-EDF public signal EDF prefix parsed; full 4,620-byte hypnogram fetched and sleep labels parsed. Open Data Commons Attribution License 1.0 on PhysioNet; not CC BY. Full nights were not downloaded. Historical cohorts and scorer labels do not establish clinical benefit; check model/software terms before redistributing weights.

CPU plan
Pilot: four to eight nights, cached epoch features and tiny gate; process EDF in chunks, target below 4 GB RAM. Scale to a participant-stratified subset of 20–40 people before deciding whether all nights are necessary.

Semester scope
Weeks 1–3: validate epochs, stages and participant grouping Weeks 4–8: reproduce unsmoothed/fixed and current quality-aware alternatives Weeks 9–14: train gate, compare equal context and test duration sensitivities Weeks 15–20: ablate signal quality versus confidence, quantify uncertainty and write

Closest work
StageGuard (July 2026) already combines transition constraints, duration models and signal quality. October duration-aware world-model work and May random-transformer research make generic learned smoothing a weak novelty claim. AnySleep (October 30) addresses broad channel-agnostic staging at a very different training scale.

Proposed contribution
An applied failure-analysis and mitigation project centred on preserving labelled short bouts under a fixed computation/context budget. A careful negative result against simple/current smoothers is legitimate; superiority is not promised.

Research connection
Health Intelligence studies learning from noninvasive EEG and interpretation of brain activity. This thesis concerns the reliability of EEG-derived temporal decisions.

Limits
LTU page focuses inner speech and neurological/BCI work; no specific sleep-staging project or clinical collaborator is assumed. Proposed fit only: no examiner, supervisor, project membership or resource approval.

Risks
Gate may reproduce StageGuard rather than add useful behaviour Few short bouts yield wide intervals Sleep EDF label conventions and cohorts limit generalization

Output
A reproducible scorer comparison, trained correction gate and an evidence-based account of which short changes are lost by smoothing.

Verdict
Data and CPU path pass, but exact scope should be tightened against very close existing work; LTU brain-signal methods fit is adjacent rather than an advertised sleep project.
