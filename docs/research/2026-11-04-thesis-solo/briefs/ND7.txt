ND7: Should an ECG model fill missing signals or work with what it has?
4 November 2026 — KEEP

Problem
Reconstructed ECG channels can look plausible while changing the classification result.

Example
Two recorded ECG lead channels are unavailable. Compare rebuilding those signals with telling the classifier which channels are missing. This does not simulate the physical effects of electrode failure.

Research question
At a matched CPU/model budget, when does direct mask-aware multi-label classification outperform reconstruction-then-classification under missing ECG leads and time segments?

What you would build/train
Train a compact 1-D CNN at 100 Hz with observed-signal masks and structured lead dropout. Compare linear/compact autoencoder reconstruction followed by the same classifier. Learn calibration on held-out patients.

Comparisons
Zero-fill plus missingness mask; fixed surviving-lead classifier Linear cross-lead regression and small denoising autoencoder Direct structured-dropout classifier; ImputeECG as the current high-compute reconstruction reference, not a promised reproduction

Evaluation
Use PTB-XL v1.0.3 patient-wise folds 1–8/9/10, with duplicate audit. Mask leads/time windows only after splitting. Evaluate macro-AUPRC, label-wise sensitivity at preselected specificity, calibration and risk/coverage. Separate random failures from lead-specific structured loss. Synthetic missingness is not evidence about a clinic’s actual failure process.

Data and terms
Public PTB-XL metadata prefix parsed, complete waveform header read, and full 24,000-byte 100-Hz 12-lead ECG record decoded. CC BY 4.0. No institutional credentials/new agreement required for these files. Clinical deployment, patient benefit and external-hospital validity are not established.

CPU plan
Pilot: 1,000–2,000 records, five diagnostic superclasses, less than 500k parameters, short early-stopped runs. Stream batches; target under 6 GB RAM. Expand only after measuring wall time; no A100 replication or 500-Hz transformer training.

Semester scope
Weeks 1–3: validate labels, folds and signal scaling Weeks 4–8: train matched compact baselines Weeks 9–14: structured missingness and direct-versus-reconstruction ablations Weeks 15–20: calibration, subgroup uncertainty, compute profile and thesis

Closest work
ImputeECG (July 2026) already tests diagnosis after reconstruction and warns about unrecoverable transient abnormalities. Its A100 training and external/private evaluations are outside this plan.

Proposed contribution
An equal-budget applied comparison of directly learned missingness handling against reconstruction, including where visually good reconstruction harms decisions. Downstream diagnostic evaluation itself is not new.

Research connection
LTU ML staff explicitly work on physiological-signal-driven interaction and reliable intelligent systems; the study contributes robust learning under missing physiological measurements.

Limits
The verified Health Intelligence projects concentrate on brain signals. This is a proposed extension, not a verified LTU ECG project or available medical supervision. Proposed fit only: no examiner, supervisor, project membership or resource approval.

Risks
July closest work is outside the desired 90-day corroboration window; no invented fresh trigger Full-waveform public labels are coarse Missingness simulation cannot prove safety of reconstructed signals

Output
Compact trained ECG models and a reproducible decision-level comparison explaining when reconstruction is unnecessary or harmful.

Verdict
Verified public signal/label access and an achievable, substantive applied model-development/evaluation study. Rank below the directly LTU-linked voice/inner-speech directions for institutional specificity.
